## Design Context

### Users

**Primary audience.** Technical decision-makers evaluating Thulani for senior
contract work: engineering managers, platform leads, CTOs, and recruiters at
South African (and increasingly remote-EU/UK) banks, financial services, and
enterprise IT teams. They land here from LinkedIn, a referral, or a recruiter
shortlist. They are skimming on a laptop during work hours — not browsing for
delight, **auditing for credibility in under 60 seconds.**

**Secondary audience.** Peer engineers reading deep on architecture posts or
project write-ups, often after-hours, dark room, headphones on.

**Job to be done.** *"Can this person be trusted to own a regulated-industry
Kubernetes platform or .NET microservice without supervision?"* Every pixel
should answer yes. The site is a **trust artifact**, not a portfolio
playground.

**Context.** Desktop-first (recruiters and managers on work machines), with a
real mobile audience for LinkedIn click-throughs. Read in well-lit offices
during the day, dim rooms at night — both themes must be first-class.

### Brand Personality

**Three words.** *Composed. Operator. Specific.*

- **Composed** — quiet authority. Doesn't shout. Doesn't sell. The work does.
- **Operator** — someone who runs systems in production, not someone who
  reads about them. Numbers, infrastructure diagrams, run-book language.
- **Specific** — concrete: "MicroK8s + ArgoCD, GitOps, 99.95% over 14 months,"
  not "passionate full-stack engineer." Verbs over adjectives.

**Voice.** Plain, declarative, slightly under-stated. South African
engineering register — direct, dry, no marketing puffery, no emoji. Closer
to an SRE postmortem than a startup landing page.

**Emotional goal.** The reader should feel **reassured**. *"This person is
expensive and worth it."*

### Aesthetic Direction

**Bold direction.** Refined editorial — *engineering field-notes meets
financial-section newspaper*. Think *The Financial Times* tech column or a
well-typeset Stripe-era engineering blog, not a SaaS landing page and
absolutely not a "creative dev" personal site.

**Themes.** Both. Light is the default for daytime recruiter viewing
(serious, paper-like, generous margins). Dark is the late-night deep-read
mode (warm-neutral dark, never the cyan-on-black AI palette).

**Anti-references** (what this must NOT look like):

- Aurora / blurred-blob / glassmorphism developer portfolios. Never bring
  WebGL hero scenes back.
- Cyan-purple-pink gradient hero text. Banned outright.
- "Creative coder" portfolios with cursors, scroll-jacking, and Three.js
  hero scenes. Wrong audience signal.
- Generic shadcn-default rounded cards with the same padding everywhere.
- Reflex-list fonts (Inter, DM Sans, Fraunces, Plex, Space Grotesk,
  Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans). Use the font-selection procedure to look
  further.

**References for the right feel:**

- Editorial restraint of long-form engineering writing (Bryan Cantrill posts,
  Increment magazine layouts).
- The print-like hierarchy of a serious tech publication.
- Hardware-product manuals from the 70s/80s — confident, captioned, dense.

### Design Principles

These five principles override anything else when in conflict. If a design
choice doesn't ladder to one of these, it doesn't ship.

1. **Credibility over delight.** Every element earns its place by raising
   trust. Decoration that doesn't is removed. Animation that doesn't is
   removed. No motion-for-motion's-sake. Reduced-motion is the default
   feel, not the fallback.

2. **Specific over generic.** Real numbers, real client tier (where NDA
   allows), real architecture nouns. Replace "I build scalable systems"
   with "ran a 14-node MicroK8s cluster for an SA tier-1 bank's internal
   PaaS." Microcopy is technical, not aspirational.

3. **Typographic hierarchy carries the design.** One distinctive display
   face + one quiet body face do the heavy lifting. Neither may be Inter,
   DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Space Grotesk, Outfit, Fraunces, Playfair, or
   any other reflex font. Pick something that reads as **a serious
   publication**, not a SaaS pitch deck. Modular scale, generous leading,
   capped measure (~70ch).

4. **Asymmetric, captioned, dense — not card-grids.** Borrow from print:
   wide gutters, sidenotes, captions under figures, varied column widths.
   Skills are not a 3×3 icon grid. Projects are not equal cards. The
   experience timeline already zigzags — extend that editorial logic
   everywhere else.

5. **Neutral-warm palette, single accent, used sparingly.** Paper-warm
   neutrals tinted toward a single brand hue (warm amber / desaturated
   ochre / oxidized brass — pick one in build, not in tokens copied from
   the current violet+aurora system). One accent, ≤10% visual weight,
   reserved for active state, the one number you want read first, and
   the inline link underline. No multi-color "skill category" tinting.

### Hard Rules (derived)

- **Fonts:** Type pair is **Source Serif 4 Variable** (display) +
  **Public Sans Variable** (body) + **JetBrains Mono** (narrow technical
  use only — code blocks, tabular numerics). Future passes must use the
  font-selection procedure if revisiting; never reach back to the
  reflex list.
- **Color:** OKLCH tokens. Neutrals tinted toward the brand hue.
  No pure `#000` / `#fff`. No gradient text. No cyan-on-dark.
- **Brand-accent surface area cap:** the accent hue may not paint
  display-sized text (h1 hero name, oversized numerics). The accent is
  reserved for: active state, link underline, the `Maseko` underline,
  the "Open to work" pill, the photo hairline ring, scroll progress,
  skill-toggle active. Total visual weight ≤10%.
- **Dark mode is warm-coal, not cool-black.** Dark `--background` is
  `oklch(0.18 0.012 50)`. Future passes must not regress toward
  cyan/blue-tinted dark backgrounds.
- **`--secondary` is intentionally close to `--muted`** in OKLCH
  (single-accent design direction). Do not use `text-secondary` /
  `bg-secondary` as a chromatic signal — they read monochrome by
  design. If a component needs chromatic emphasis, use `text-primary`
  (the single coral accent) or introduce a new semantic token with
  explicit justification.
- **Layout:** No card-everything. Use rules, captions, sidenotes, and
  asymmetric columns. `gap` over margins. 4pt spacing scale with
  semantic names.
- **Motion:** Page-load orchestration only. No scroll-triggered fireworks.
  Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` as the *target* aesthetic, not the
  degraded one. Use `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` for height
  transitions; never animate `height: auto`.
- **Aurora background:** retired. `PaperBackground.tsx` (column rules +
  masthead hairline + folio mark) is the canonical hero backdrop.
  WebGL hero scenes do not return.
- **Glassmorphism:** retired. `.glass` and `.glass-card` are flat paper
  surfaces with hairline borders, no `backdrop-filter`. Future
  components must not reintroduce blur backdrops.
- **Decorative marks yield to functional widgets.** If two elements
  compete for the same corner, the functional/informational one wins;
  the decorative one moves.
- **All bans** in `~/.claude/skills/impeccable/SKILL.md` `<absolute_bans>`
  apply, in particular: no `border-left` accent stripes, no gradient text.
